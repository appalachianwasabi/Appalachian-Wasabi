Grating fresh wasabi, start to finish

October 15, 2026

A fine grater, slow circles and a fifteen-minute window: how to get the most out of a fresh rhizome.

The first time you grate a fresh wasabi rhizome, the aroma is the surprise. It’s bright, green and a little sweet, nothing like the sharp paste most of us grew up with. Here’s how to get the most out of it.

Prep only what you need

Rinse the rhizome under cool water and scrub it gently. Trim off any leaf stems at the top, then scrape the knobby bumps off just the end you plan to grate. Leave the rest alone; it keeps better that way.

Fine grater, slow circles

Use the finest grater you have. A sharkskin grater is traditional, but ceramic and fine metal graters work too. Hold the cut end flat and move it in slow, gentle circles. Gather the paste and let it sit for a minute.

Then eat it, quickly

Grated wasabi is at its best for roughly 10 to 15 minutes, so grate right before you sit down, and only as much as you’ll use. Put a small dab directly on fish, stir it into soba dipping sauce, or set a little beside a steak.
